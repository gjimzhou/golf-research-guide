# Difficulty and Tee Selection

“Play the tees that match your game” is more useful than choosing tees by ego, age, or a single total-yardage threshold.

## Course Rating vs. Slope

According to the USGA:

- **Course Rating** is the expected difficulty for a scratch player under normal conditions.
- **Bogey Rating** represents difficulty for a bogey player.
- **Slope Rating** measures how much the course's relative difficulty increases for non-scratch players compared with scratch players.

A higher Slope number does not automatically mean a course is absolutely harder than every course with a lower Slope. Course Rating and effective playing length also matter.

## Why yardage alone fails

Two courses of the same yardage can play very differently because of:

- elevation;
- wind;
- fairway roll;
- forced layups;
- forced carries;
- rough;
- trees;
- bunkers;
- water;
- green size and firmness;
- green contours.

USGA course-rating methodology explicitly incorporates effective playing length and obstacle factors rather than raw scorecard yardage alone.

## Vacation-golf tee selection

For destination golf, an appropriate tee usually lets the player:

- reach most par 4s in regulation with normal solid shots;
- carry mandatory hazards without needing career-best drives;
- use a range of clubs rather than driver + long club all day;
- experience the intended angles of the hole;
- keep pace without repeated searches or forced re-tees.

If a famous course is exposed to strong wind, moving forward is often a better way to preserve the architecture and enjoyment.

## A practical pre-round method

Before the trip:

1. look up the current Course Rating and Slope for each tee;
2. inspect the scorecard hole by hole, not only total yardage;
3. identify the longest forced carries;
4. identify unusually long par 3s;
5. adjust for elevation and prevailing wind;
6. choose the tee that makes the difficult holes challenging but plausible.

At the course, move again if actual wind or turf conditions make the selected tee inappropriate.

## For mixed-skill groups

There is no requirement that a group play one tee. Different players can use different tees while still playing together.

Trip quality usually improves when each player chooses a suitable tee rather than forcing the entire group onto the same markers.
