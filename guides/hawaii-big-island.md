# Hawaii — Big Island Golf

## Hualālai Golf Course

Hualālai is especially compelling when paired with a stay at Four Seasons Resort Hualālai because course access, transportation, practice facilities, and the resort day fit together with minimal friction.

The official Four Seasons page states that the Jack Nicklaus Signature course is available to club members, their registered guests, registered resort-rental guests, and Four Seasons Resort Hualālai guests.

### Twilight is a real itinerary product

Four Seasons currently markets a 2:00 PM “Twilight 18 Holes” experience and says the timing is intended to reach the oceanfront finishing holes around sunset.

The detailed 18-hole experience page adds an important operational rule: **carts should be returned 10 minutes before sunset**.

That illustrates the core lesson: nominal sunset time is not the real deadline.

### Maintenance matters

As of 2026-09-22, Four Seasons publishes this 2026 aeration schedule:

- September 14–18: one nine closed;
- September 21–25: the second nine closed;
- tees, fairways, and rough also receive maintenance during the period;
- special maintenance rates are offered.

This is exactly why current maintenance dates must be checked before paying full destination-golf pricing.

The site also already publishes 2027 closures and aeration windows, which makes early itinerary screening possible.

## Mauna Kea Golf Course

Mauna Kea is a strong standalone Big Island round and offers a useful contrast to Hualālai.

General lesson: a destination course can justify a drive when it adds a genuinely different landscape or architectural experience, but not merely to increase round count.

## Mauna Lani South Course

The South Course is often the more obvious vacation-golf choice when scenery is the priority. Lava fields, ocean exposure, and resort integration make it a natural comparison point for a memorable destination round.

## Mauna Lani North Course

The North Course provides a different golf product and can be more attractive when routing, lava-landscape character, and shot variety matter more than maximizing ocean frontage.

## Short-format golf

Short-format resort golf can be valuable rather than redundant. A few holes, par-3 loop, or practice-focused session works well for:

- a low-commitment second golf session;
- a mixed-skill group;
- swing work;
- a sunset activity;
- a day when a full round would consume too much resort time.

Four Seasons, for example, currently offers a four-hole “Taste of Hualālai Golf” experience after 4:00 PM with rental clubs included.

## Access and exclusivity

Big Island courses vary substantially in access model. Do not label a course simply “public” or “private” without checking the actual guest rule for the travel date.

Ask:

- hotel guest only?
- resort-rental guest?
- public tee times?
- reciprocal or member introduction?
- stay-and-play package?
- minimum-stay requirement?
