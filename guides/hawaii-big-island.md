# Hawaii — Big Island Golf

## Hualālai Golf Course

Hualālai is especially compelling when paired with a stay at Four Seasons Resort Hualālai because course access, transportation, practice facilities, and the resort day fit together with minimal friction.

The key planning lesson is that a discounted twilight tee time is not automatically optimal. On a scenic resort course, evaluate local sunset, expected pace, which finishing holes are visually important, cart-return/course-closing rules, probability of being paired, and downstream dinner or resort commitments.

If the goal includes seeing the closing holes in good light, an earlier afternoon start can be worth more than a cheaper late slot.

## Mauna Kea Golf Course

Mauna Kea is a strong standalone Big Island round and offers a useful contrast to Hualālai. Its coastal setting makes it attractive even when it is not attached to the hotel being used as a base.

General lesson: a destination course can justify a drive when it adds a genuinely different landscape or architectural experience, but not merely to increase round count.

## Mauna Lani South Course

The South Course is often the more obvious vacation-golf choice when scenery is the priority. Lava fields, ocean views, and resort integration make it particularly suitable for a memorable destination round.

## Mauna Lani North Course

The North Course offers a different emphasis, with more of a lava-landscape and shot-making character. It can be the better choice when golf architecture and technical variety matter more than maximizing coastal scenery.

## Wikiwiki Course

Short-format resort golf can be valuable rather than redundant. A nine-hole or walking course works well for practice, a low-commitment second session, introducing a less-experienced player, or photography/sunset without another full round.

## Access and exclusivity

Big Island courses vary substantially in access model. Some are strongly resort-oriented, others support broader public/resort inventory, and a handful of clubs are private enough that they should not be treated as realistic default itinerary options.

Always verify current access rules rather than relying on an old characterization of a course as "public" or "private."
