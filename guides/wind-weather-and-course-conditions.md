# Weather, Closures, and Course Conditions

This page summarizes issues that came up in Hawaii golf planning, especially storm recovery and maintenance risk.

## “Open” is not the same as normal

A course can reopen while still dealing with:

- storm debris;
- damaged trees;
- power or facility problems;
- restricted operations;
- aeration recovery;
- temporary maintenance conditions.

That distinction mattered in the discussion of Princeville Makai after a major storm.

## Verify status before the cancellation deadline

When a premium course is an important part of the itinerary, recheck:

- whether the course is open;
- whether all holes are operating normally;
- aeration or maintenance dates;
- storm-recovery status;
- cart restrictions if relevant;
- cancellation or rain-check policy.

## Weather can change the hotel or course decision

When golf is one reason for choosing a specific part of an island or a specific hotel, a major disruption can justify re-planning more than just the tee time.

That was the recurring logic in the Kauai discussions: course recovery, hotel plans, and cancellation windows had to be considered together.

## Build in flexibility when uncertainty is high

Refundable bookings and alternative activities become more valuable when the status of a course is uncertain.

The point is not to predict weather perfectly. It is to avoid locking the whole trip around an activity that may not be operating normally.

## Scope

Do not expand this page into a general meteorology, wind-modeling, rainfall-analysis, or course-conditioning manual unless those topics are separately researched in conversation.
